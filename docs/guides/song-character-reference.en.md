# Song dynasty character and costume references

[简体中文](song-character-reference.md) · [Dynasty production guide](dynasty-reference-workflow.en.md)

Use this example to plan combinations. Reuse inner layers across seasons and add outerwear for a distinct construction. Produce individual images in authorized batches.

## Scope and sources

This guide covers the Northern Song (960–1127) and Southern Song (1127–1279), primarily cities and rural communities in Han Chinese regions. Place, social position, and date affect dress. A burial documents a particular context. Contemporary Liao, Jin, and Western Xia subjects need their own sources and entries.

- A reconstruction of a *beizi* jacket and a two-panel wrap skirt from Lady Zhou’s Southern Song tomb gives structural evidence for one woman’s burial garments. [China National Silk Museum](https://www.chinasilkmuseum.com/yg/info_13.aspx?itemid=31106)
- The museum’s unlined plain gauze garment provides evidence for material and excavation context within the scope of that object. [China National Silk Museum](https://www.chinasilkmuseum.com/gskt/info_319.aspx?itemid=28354)
- An exhibition on ancient Chinese dress places Song material alongside dress from contemporary states. [National Museum of China](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/portals/0/web/zt/202102gdfsh/)
- Research on *Along the River During the Qingming Festival* provides visual leads for Northern Song urban life. Allow for the artist’s choices when interpreting dress. [Palace Museum](https://www.dpm.org.cn/forum_detail/99722.html)
- Clothing from Zhao Boyun’s tomb documents a Southern Song scholar-official of imperial descent. Its scope is narrower than ordinary urban dress. [Zhejiang Federation of Social Sciences](https://www.zjskw.gov.cn/art/2022/3/18/art_1229514868_45741.html)

These entries are design proposals. Terms such as “inn attendant” and “maid” aid search. Wandering heroes and night travelers are creative roles. Gender labels specify the intended designs. Verify officeholding, women’s occupations, clothing rules, colors, ornaments, and ceremonial rank against specific evidence.

## Seasonal layers

Adapt each suggestion to local weather, class, and working conditions. Store inner and outer garments separately and reuse identical cuts. The table describes styling options.

| Season | Inner layers | Outer and lower garments | Footwear and movement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spring | Light inner shirt with trousers or underskirt, tidy collar and cuffs | Unlined shirt, shirt and skirt, or tunic and trousers, with a light overgarment as appropriate | Cloth shoes, sleeves and trouser cuffs suited to work |
| Summer | Light, breathable shirt with trousers or underskirt | Fewer lined layers and lighter fabric, with ceremony-specific layering | Light shoes, checking material against status |
| Autumn | Medium-weight shirt with trousers or underskirt | Add a lined layer or overshirt to the basic outfit | Cloth shoes or boots according to role and occasion |
| Winter | Heavier shirt with trousers or underskirt | Lined or padded garment, warm overshirt, and an additional wrap where needed | Warm shoes, with protective military equipment separate from insulation |

Use plain inner shirts, undertrousers, or underskirts by default. Research women’s close-fitting undergarments separately using surviving objects to establish names, construction, and how they were worn together.

## Representative roles

“Basic” means the separate inner shirt and undertrousers or underskirt above. The default period is Song-themed, with a narrower date to be established. Reuse the same cut across roles and seasons where appropriate.

## Pose, hair, and view planning

- Initial actions include relaxed standing, walking, formal sitting, greeting, adjusting sleeves, holding a book, carrying a tray, using a shoulder pole, holding tools, leading a horse, and standing guard. Tag props separately and research formal gestures.
- Initial head references include tied hair, cloth wraps, ordinary coiled buns, plain pins, scholars’ cloth caps, official caps, ceremonial crowns, helmets, and child styles. Verify terms and contexts before attributing rank.
- This historical batch specified front, three-quarter, side, and back PNGs for each selected pose, inner layer, outer garment, and head asset. Deliver separate files. For new poses, use the [current camera angles and sample portrait requirements](../../.agents/skills/hanfu-asset-library/SKILL.en.md).
- Suggested fields are dynasty, subperiod, region, role, type, presentation, age, setting, season, component, name, construction, sources, image status, and review notes.

## Example combination

For an adult Southern Song maid working in spring, combine a simple bun tied with cloth, a plain short work top and long skirt, a light inner shirt and underskirt, and a standing tray-carrying pose. Choose the corresponding view of each component. This is a proposed portrait composition.

## Delivery status for the documented batch

The Song batch had **43 sets and 172 independent views** across 25 outer garments, 2 year-round inner sets, and 16 headpieces or buns. People were removed from 18 retained sets. Additions comprised 15 outer garments and 10 role-specific headpieces. At the user’s request, the 7 retired sets, 18 earlier human versions, obsolete overview, and archived metadata were permanently removed.

New garments included plain robes, long and short lamellar armor, Buddhist daily/work/ceremonial clothing, Daoist men’s and women’s dress, women’s work trousers, men’s and women’s dance and music outfits, boys’ and girls’ clothes, and ceremonial outfits for emperors and princes. Head assets included a winged *futou*, *tongtian* crown, ridged crown, helmet, court floral pins, high bun with comb and pins, work cloth, travel hat, small Daoist crown, and performance bun. A monk’s shaved head needed no standalone human-head image.

The overview and completion list, role combinations, and seasonal coverage tracked this batch. Role candidates followed the planned genders and reused outerwear by purpose. Innerwear was shared across roles and seasons. Child outerwear was available, while dedicated child innerwear had yet to be made at this stage. Adult sizes were unsuitable substitutes. Remaining seasonal gaps were recorded as such.

C054 and C058 are creative robes with an open layered front and a closed round neck respectively. Neither has been verified as a Song imperial ceremonial robe. C320/H065 draws on the combination of a crimson gauze court robe, a square-centered curved collar, and a *tongtian* crown. Motifs, crown shape, rank, and precise date still need review.
