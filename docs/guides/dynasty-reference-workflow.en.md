# Building a dynasty reference library

Select gender presentations according to period, occupation, and the role under study. The library ordinarily plans male soldiers, government runners, inn attendants, and merchant escorts. Across the six dynasties it does not commission separate women’s work trousers or women soldiers’ uniforms by default. Women doing everyday work can use suitable clothing already available for that period. Deliberately removed designs do not count as gaps.

## Reviewing existing and new assets

1. **Find comparable looks.** For each design, look in at least two classic productions set in the relevant period. Match subperiod, region, identity, age, and occasion. Inspect individual frames. If none match, record the shows searched, queries, and findings, then follow step 6. Record production, character, source page, and visible parts. Choose one complete look as the main reference. A half-length shot supports only the visible upper body. Record the basis for the back and inner layers separately.
2. **Review the whole outfit and catalog its parts.** Check dominant and accent colors on the body, collar, and sleeves, layering, sheen, hair, headwear, ornaments, and shoes. Preserve visible color and fabric distinctions even in plain clothes. The four garment or headwear views must depict the same design. Remove bodies and stands. Shoes may remain. Compare shoes only when visible in both the asset and existing still. Earlier reviews need not reopen for footwear alone.
3. **Deduplicate within each dynasty.** Compare seasonal sets for the same role and purpose side by side across all four angles. Keep one design when collar, opening, sleeves, silhouette, layers, and status markers closely match. Record applicable seasons as tags. Keep another design when an independent jacket, armor, fur collar, or thick lining visibly changes its construction. Color, season names, or differences in prompt wording alone do not justify another set.
4. **Keep the best representative.** Prefer the closest source match with complete, consistent views and legible details. Migrate role links and verified seasonal and period references before removing redundant entries and their exclusive files. Record retired-to-retained IDs and verify files and role coverage. Apply step 6 if stills are unavailable.
5. **Resolve findings in the same batch.** For clothing, innerwear, hair, headwear, and poses, edit or replace confirmed errors in palette, structure, combination, action, or presentation. Merge or remove redundant designs under step 4. For a representative role or use with no suitable reusable asset, produce and catalog the missing set. The original garment workflow delivered four individual views. Use the current delivery specification above for newly generated views and pose angles. Seasonal tag counts alone do not justify production.
6. **Review the delivered result.** With limited or no direct stills, consult period objects or images, similar roles and uses, then verified comparable library assets, in that order. Compare the four views and resolve the entry by retaining, editing, merging, or removing it. Record inferred details, reasons, and actual sources as a creative inference. Keep a matching image, revise a discrepant one, or generate a representative missing design. Missing stills alone need not leave a case open. Inspect each delivered angle, update the catalog, role links, review status, and previews, then check image paths and coverage. Report actual replacements, additions, merges, deletions, and reasons for outstanding checks separately.

**After completing the item-by-item audit**, compare the same role across periods. Match age, purpose, cut, wearing method, status markers, and accessories against each period’s sources. Mark a representative design as shared only for verified periods. Keep distinct rank badges, crowns, hairstyles, or ritual elements. If the garment is shared but the cap differs, merge only the garment.

## Default approach

Inventory representative roles and reusable cuts before scheduling small batches. Role coverage, seasonal styling plans, and unique cuts are separate measures. A role, gender, season, and layer combination does not require its own image. Apply the method to Han, Song, Ming, and other periods using their own evidence. A representative collection covers the agreed scope. It cannot establish every historical clothing form.

This guide directs a human or assistant. The website does not authenticate historical reconstructions. In the Tang batch, non-core garments were archived and curated garments converted to garment-only views. Eight children’s sets were restored at the user’s request, converted with child proportions, and counted by cut. Other dynasties were handled in later batches.

## Color and materials for newly made clothing

Depict freshly made, clean garments. Avoid giving every historical fabric a yellow, faded, hazy, or excavated appearance. Retain colors appropriate to role, rank, and occasion, including genuinely dark and subdued clothes. Preserve the material and social context of workwear and officials’ robes.

- **Court and embroidered clothing.** Preserve clear color relationships, believable silk reflections, and legible metallic thread.
- **Everyday and child clothing.** Distinguish cotton, hemp, and silk. Avoid defaulting to `dusty`, `faded`, or `aged`. Keep intentional gray or plain designs.
- **Workwear, everyday religious clothing, and armor.** Preserve simple materials and legible shadows. Color alone cannot establish office or rank.
- **Innerwear.** Preserve natural white, ivory, and pale colors. Avoid clipped highlights and excessive saturation.
- Apply consistent changes to all four files so downloads match the preview.

## 1. Define period and sources

Specify dynasty, subperiod, region, and setting. If given only a dynasty, identify major chronological differences and select one clearly labeled representative scope first. Use museum objects, archaeological reports, contemporary imagery, and dress regulations. Record the scope of each source and distinguish evidence, research inference, and period-inspired design.

Distinguish historical status, familiar search terms, and literary or screen characters. *Gege* is inappropriate for Song. Describe Tang or Song merchant escorts as creative characters unless the institution and uniform are documented. The same garment name across periods does not establish the same construction. Add a subperiod variant only where structure or regulations differ materially.

## 3. Catalog designs by construction

### Outerwear

Consider a new garment for a distinct collar or closure, sleeve cut or length, overall silhouette, skirt or trouser structure, layering, insulation, protective equipment, or documented rank decoration absent from existing assets. Compare the images themselves. A different name, file hash, prompt, face, color, season tag, or invisible material claim cannot establish a unique cut. Retain distinct official dress, ceremonial wear, armor, and religious clothing even when all are long.

Record reusable roles and restrictions. Match era, gender presentation, occasion, status, structure, and layering before sharing. A plain everyday robe may fit several roles. Court or official clothing needs its own justification.

### Seasons

Season tags describe suitability. A category may contain one design or cover only some seasons. Preserve empty matches unless a distinct necessary use or construction is missing. Assign all-season tags only with support.

| Type | Keep when | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light single layer | The basic cut works across spring and summer with only color or fabric wording changing | Spring and summer, adjusted for region |
| Layered | A vest or jacket visibly changes silhouette or layers | Spring, autumn, or cool weather |
| Insulated | Padding, shoulder covering, fur collar, or cloak changes construction | Winter or cold regions |
| Ceremonial or occupational | Occasion, regulations, or equipment require a distinct structure | Across suitable seasons |

Several seasonal plans can cite one ID and describe layers added or removed. Check that a shared cloak or jacket physically fits the outfit.

### Inner garments

Share inner layers by cut. Distinguish short and long inner shirts, collars, sleeves, ties, undertrousers, underskirts, and wrapped garments. Document genuinely different insulation or protection as a functional design.

Inspect a *dudou* separately. Its front chest-and-abdomen panel, neck tie, and rear waist ties differ from a long apron over an inner shirt and from the Song chest wrap. Establish period and structure first. Plan child sizes where necessary. Embroidery and color alone do not create new cuts. I011 is a creative long apron-style layer and does not establish coverage of a standalone *dudou*. Evidence for one Qing design has a limited historical scope. [National Museum of China research](https://www.chnmuseum.cn/yj/xscg/xslw/201812/t20181224_36509.shtml)

Label wearing examples separately. An inner shirt tucked into a skirt waist illustrates styling. An ornate complete outer ensemble mislabeled as innerwear should not be retained as core underwear. Where needed, create fully covering templates in child proportions.

### Hair, headwear, and poses

Give head assets and poses separate IDs and choices from garments. Plan structurally distinct hairstyles for children, adolescents, young adults, middle-aged adults, and older adults without repeating them by role and season. Restrict official hats, ceremonial crowns, and helmets by period, status, and occasion. Reuse standing, sitting, walking, working, ceremonial, and occupational poses. Record props separately.

| Coverage | Inspect | Reuse or separate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ruler | Everyday or major ceremony | Verify informal and ceremonial headwear against the period’s system |
| Heir and imperial family | Crown prince, titled imperial son, prince | Distinguish succession, title, and occasion before assigning a crown |
| Civil officials | Everyday, audience, formal rites | Share ordinary hats where documented. Restrict formal crowns by rank and occasion |
| Military | Commanders, soldiers, protection | Separate structurally distinct helmets. Color and gold trim alone cannot establish rank |
| Court and noble women | Empress, consorts, imperial kinswomen at ceremonies or celebrations | Distinguish ornate buns from ritual crowns. Subdivide by rank, marriage, or occasion with evidence |
| Town and household | Merchant, attendant, farmer, artisan, servant, maid | Share plain buns, pins, and wraps where appropriate |
| Buddhist and Daoist | Monks, abbots, priests, priestesses | An abbot needs no new basic head shape. Record a shaved head as hair or pose information. Verify religious crowns by period |
| Travel and performance | Travelers, escorts, musicians, dancers | Share hats and ribbons by use. Label screen designs |
| Age | Children, adolescents, adults, older adults | Keep structurally distinct age-appropriate styles and check role-specific headwear separately |

Check wearing space before combining a bun and headpiece. Older hairstyle references with faces can retain their historical status. New assets require the object-only specification and actual image edits to qualify.

## 4. Plan first and generate in small batches

1. Inventory catalog entries, images, and earlier curation lists. Identify existing, questionable, and genuinely absent designs.
2. Map representative roles and standard gender options to distinct cuts. Mark each candidate for reuse, addition, or deferral with a reason.
3. Prioritize missing basic cuts and shared innerwear, followed by distinctive ceremonial, occupational, military, and seasonal constructions. Add exceptional roles, child sizes, or screen variants as needed.
4. Follow the requested scope and count. When generation is authorized without a count, use 4–8 distinct sets per batch. Planning or summary requests deliver a plan only.
5. Compare completed sets with the library. Fix quality problems before adding more. A duplicate does not need more colors, seasonal labels, or gender variants.
6. Continue with the next distinct gaps on the authorized list. Completion of one batch does not extend its scope to other dynasties or roles.

## 8. Reusable request

For each dynasty, deliver six parts in order.

1. **Period brief.** Dynasty, subperiod, region, sources, and any out-of-period or screen-specific titles.
2. **Role coverage.** Roles, standard gender options, ages, occasions, reusable cuts, headwear types, and status restrictions. Cover the five major role groups, religious figures, and children.
3. **Asset lists.** Separate outerwear, shared innerwear, and head assets. Record construction, evidence and open questions, meaningful differences, existing IDs, reuse/add/defer decisions, and image status.
4. **Seasons and combinations.** List verified seasons and candidates for each outer category. Inner layers are season-independent. Give at least one compatible combination for each major role and seasonal alternatives where applicable.
5. **Batch queue.** Order genuine gaps by priority. Use 4–8 sets or the user’s count. Check views and duplicates before starting the next batch.
6. **Acceptance counts.** Report role coverage, outer and inner sets, head sets, actual views, gaps, repairs, and archived replacements. Assess image quality, object-only presentation, and historical evidence independently.

Combination fields are dynasty/subperiod, role, gender/age, occasion, season, inner ID, outer ID, optional overlayer, bun/headwear ID, optional pose/prop, compatibility limits, and gaps. Assign a plan ID to unproduced items. Mark hidden or built-in layers to prevent double layering. A combination record describes a proposal until the portrait has been generated.
